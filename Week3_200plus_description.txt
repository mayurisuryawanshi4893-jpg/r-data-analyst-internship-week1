This Week 3 project performs statistical analysis and predictive modeling using R on the IBM Telco Customer Churn dataset. The dataset was continued from Weeks 1 and 2 to maintain a consistent analytical workflow. After converting TotalCharges to numeric and removing records with missing values, 7,032 customer observations were available for analysis. The target variable is Churn, a binary outcome indicating whether a customer left the service.

The statistical analysis includes hypothesis testing, correlation analysis, and distribution checks. A Welch two-sample t-test was used to test whether average MonthlyCharges differs between churned and non-churned customers. A chi-square test was used to evaluate the relationship between Contract type and Churn. Pearson correlation was calculated between tenure and TotalCharges to measure the strength and direction of their linear association. The R script also includes a normality check and model diagnostics.

For predictive modeling, binary logistic regression was selected because the target variable has two classes. Predictors include tenure, MonthlyCharges, TotalCharges, Contract, InternetService, PaymentMethod, PaperlessBilling, SeniorCitizen, Partner, and Dependents. The data was divided into training and testing sets using a stratified 80/20 split. Model performance was evaluated using accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, ROC-AUC, a confusion matrix, and five-fold cross-validation.

The project also includes diagnostic visualizations such as a confusion matrix, ROC curve, and predicted-probability distribution. The results provide an interpretable baseline for identifying customers at risk of churn. Potential improvements include regularized logistic regression, Random Forest or gradient boosting models, feature engineering, probability-threshold optimization, class-imbalance handling, calibration, and external validation. Overall, the project demonstrates a complete workflow from statistical hypothesis formulation through predictive model evaluation and interpretation.
